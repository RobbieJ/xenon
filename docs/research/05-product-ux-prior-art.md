# Research: product UX and prior art for a two-phone, two-AirPods offline conversation app

*Research date: 10 September 2026. Sources are linked inline. Confidence levels are the researcher's own. Trademark checks were not carried out.*

Takeaways: every working iOS product uses **MultipeerConnectivity (AWDL)**, not BLE, for audio; the two BLE-audio apps (Murmur, Bitchat PTT) have no reviews on latency. The recurring complaints are discovery failures, headset routing bugs, ads/subscriptions, and background death. **Apple's Push to Talk framework is not an option**: it requires an APNs backend, so it needs the internet ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/06/08/ios-16-api-enables-walkie-talkie-for-apps/), [WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10117/)).

## 2. Apple's built-ins and the zero-code baseline

- **Live Listen**: iPhone mic → AirPods; iOS 14.3+; one-directional, ~10 m ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102479), [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/use-iphone-remote-mic-airpods-how/)). Community estimate of latency: ~150 ms on iPhone 13, ~250 ms on iPhone 6, "no way to completely eliminate" ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254988449)). Confidence: medium on the numbers.
- **Conversation Boost** (AirPods Pro 2/3, Transparency sub-setting): ~7 dB SNR gain for a talker in front; buried under Accessibility > Headphone Accommodations ([Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/hearing-products/hearing-aids/psap/apple-airpods-pro-for-people-with-hearing-loss-in-noisy-environments), [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/760431/how-to-enable-conversation-boost-on-airpods-pro/)).
- **Hearing Aid feature** (AirPods Pro 2/3, iOS 18+, 18+, FDA De Novo authorised): reviewers say transparency "gets muddy and warbly" with several talkers and it is "not for crowded restaurants"; settings navigation "not intuitive" ([Techlicious](https://www.techlicious.com/review/apple-airpods-pro-2-as-hearing-aids-review/), [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120992)).
- **Live Translation conversation mode** (iOS 26, iPhone 15 Pro+, AirPods 4 ANC/Pro 2/3): the closest precedent for "two people, two phones, two AirPods". Notably **the phones do not link**; each iPhone independently listens via its owner's AirPods, and ANC "automatically lowers the volume of the other speaker" — one user found that still "a little too loud". Latency 1.5–4.7 s, "no fluency in conversation" ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/ios-26-use-live-translation-with-airpods/), [Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/airpods/translate-in-person-conversations-dev9c215ca94/web), [SAN](https://san.com/cc/airpods-pro-3-introduces-live-translation-but-how-accurate-is-it/)). Confidence: high.
- **Apple Watch Walkie-Talkie**: FaceTime-based, needs Wi-Fi/cellular ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108416)). Not a competitor offline.

**The cross-pairing trick (A's AirPods on B's phone + Live Listen, and vice versa).** It works in principle: Live Listen streams whichever phone the AirPods are paired to. Practical limits (confidence: medium; no published test found):
1. Pairing friction: hold the case button, pair via Bluetooth settings, lose automatic switching; H2 AirPods tied to another Apple Account raise Find My prompts; the owner's Hearing Aid/audiogram profile stays on their own phone.
2. Both phones must lie on the table near each speaker's mouth; the iPhone mic is omnidirectional and adds restaurant noise, whereas AirPods mics sit at the mouth with beamforming/Voice Isolation ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/airpods/devac96438de/web)).
3. ~150 ms one-way, no mute, no level control, no "who's talking" cue, no captions, and each phone is captured for the session.
4. Live Listen is per-AirPods; neither phone can also do anything useful.

What it teaches: the value of the app is not the audio path (Apple already has one) but **frictionless, remembered pairing; mouth-mounted mics; mute/level/quality UI; captions; and running with phones in pockets**.

## 3. Pairing and session UX patterns

- **AirDrop/NameDrop**: proximity (inches) + both unlocked + explicit consent on each side; no automatic transfer ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/personal-safety/secure-namedrop-ips97e16d3b1/web)). NFC/NameDrop is not exposed to third parties.
- **Quick Share**: BLE discovery → Wi-Fi Direct transfer; receiver must accept; "Everyone" visibility auto-expires after 10 min ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9286773?hl=en)). Time-boxed visibility is worth copying.
- **Nearby Interaction** needs its token exchanged over another channel (usually MPC), so it is not a discovery primitive ([WWDC22](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10008/)).
- **MultipeerConnectivity**: needs `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription` (with a concrete example) and `NSBonjourServices`; triggers the Local Network prompt; stops advertising/browsing in the background ([Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/651842), [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/ios/collusion-ios-multipeerconnectivity)). Both-phones-open race: objc.io recommends "transceiving" plus leader election — include uptime (or a random nonce) in invitation context and always join the older/lower peer ([objc.io](https://www.objc.io/issues/18-games/multipeer-connectivity-for-games/)).
- **Wi-Fi Aware + DeviceDiscoveryUI (iOS 26, iPhone 12+)**: publisher shows `DevicePairingView` with a PIN; browser picks the device in `DevicePicker` and enters the PIN; pairing persists in Settings > Privacy & Security > Paired Devices; later "your app can securely reconnect whenever your devices are in close proximity and actively running your application" with no PIN; `.realtime` performance mode + `.interactiveVoice` service class exists for exactly this ([WWDC25 228](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/228/), [forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797170)). Confidence: high.

**Recommended flow (fewest taps that passes review):**
First use: both open the app → each phone is already publishing *and* browsing for known partners. One person taps **Pair** (browser); the other's screen shows the system PIN sheet automatically. Race handling: if both tap Pair, the phone with the lower random session nonce silently demotes to publisher and shows "Waiting for [partner] — only one of you needs to tap." Consent is the system PIN sheet, which satisfies Apple's privacy expectations without a custom permission story. Repeat use: open app (or tap the Live Activity) → auto-reconnect to any paired partner in range → audio starts, one tap total. Fallback for pre-iOS 26 devices: MPC with a Keychain-stored partner key exchanged on first pairing, auto-accept when the key matches, and an explicit "Forget partner".

## 4. Conversation UX in noise

- **Own voice**: hearing-aid literature says own-voice delay is noticed at 3–5 ms, objectionable >10 ms, and speech production is affected >30 ms; commercial hearing aids run 8–12 ms ([Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/practice-building/practice-management/acceptable-processing-delay-in-digital-hearing-aids), [Canadian Audiologist](https://canadianaudiologist.ca/hearing-aid-delay-feature/)). Any Bluetooth path is 10× that, so **never synthesise sidetone in the app**; rely on the AirPods' own occlusion handling (ANC feedback mic partly cancels the "boomy" occluded voice) and tell users a slightly muffled own voice is normal ([IEEE/ACM TASLP](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/TASLP.2021.3130966)). Confidence: high.
- **Remote voice**: ITU-T G.114 — <150 ms one-way is "transparent", 150–400 ms acceptable, >400 ms unacceptable ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.114-200305-I%21%21PDF-E)). Lip-sync detectability is roughly 45 ms audio-lead / 125 ms audio-lag (ITU-R BT.1359; confidence medium, from memory). Because ANC only attenuates the direct voice, the user hears two copies; the HeardThat experience shows the echo is masked in loud rooms and at distance, so the product is *better* in the noisy conditions it targets. Budget: AirPods mic (HFP) + link + AirPods out. One developer measured A2DP output ~163 ms vs HFP ~9 ms but only ~30 ms real gain ([Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256097737)) — force the voice-chat (HFP) route at both ends and target <150 ms total; measure early.
- **Mute vs always-on**: always-on full duplex is the point; provide a single big mute (and AirPods stem press via the call route) rather than PTT, which competitors use only because of MPC/PTT-API limits.
- **Visual cues**: partner-speaking pulse, link quality, battery of both AirPods; a Live Activity in the Dynamic Island (compact: partner initial + level; expanded: mute/end) — Live Activities cap at 8 h, fine for dinner/flights ([WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10194/)). Use CallKit so the lock screen shows a real call with mute/end and the audio session survives locking — DirectTalk already does this.
- **Conversation Awareness / Adaptive Audio**: when the wearer speaks it lowers media volume and flips to Transparency, and users report distracting mode-flapping in exactly this scenario ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104979), [Apple Community](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256095327)). Apps cannot disable it; on session start show a one-time card: "Turn off Conversation Awareness and use Noise Cancellation (not Adaptive) for this session." Confidence: high that it interferes with media; medium for the HFP/call route.

## 5. Aircraft constraints

- FAA (2013) and EASA (2014) let airlines allow PEDs gate-to-gate with cellular off; short-range Bluetooth and Wi-Fi are permitted where the airline's safety assessment allows ([FAA](https://www.faa.gov/speeches/portable-electronic-devices-presser), [EASA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/newsroom-and-events/press-releases/easa-allows-electronic-devices-remain-and-connected-throughout)). Delta: gate-to-gate; United and BA: above 10,000 ft / not during taxi, take-off, landing ([CabinZero](https://www.cabinzero.com/blogs/packing-tips/can-you-use-wireless-bluetooth-headphones-on-planes)). Confidence: medium (secondary sources).
- iOS: Airplane Mode "turns off all radios except Bluetooth"; Wi-Fi can be re-enabled and iOS remembers the choice ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108785)). AirDrop/AWDL works in Airplane Mode with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on ([AirDroid](https://www.airdroid.com/file-transfer/airdrop-work-without-wi-fi/)).
- No regulation found that distinguishes peer-to-peer Wi-Fi (AWDL/Wi-Fi Aware) from infrastructure Wi-Fi; to crew it is just "Wi-Fi on". Design implication: detect Airplane Mode with Wi-Fi off and offer a BLE fallback (lower quality, PTT-grade) plus a one-tap "Turn Wi-Fi on" deep link. Confidence: medium-low.

## 6. Accessibility and inclusion

The app is effectively a personal remote-mic system, which is what people with mild-to-moderate loss already hack together with Live Listen and HeardThat. Follow HIG accessibility: never rely on sound alone, provide captions/transcripts and haptics for events ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [WWDC25 316](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/316/)). Apple's iOS **Live Captions** already captions third-party app audio on device ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111765), [Kent State](https://www.kent.edu/equalaccess/news/accessibility-apple-features-part-2-4-live-captions)); an in-app caption strip using the on-device SpeechAnalyzer API (iOS 26, [WWDC25 277](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/); confidence medium) on the *incoming* stream gives a cleaner speaker-labelled transcript. Avoid the words "hearing aid" in marketing: software hearing aids are now an FDA device class (874.3335) and Apple needed De Novo authorisation ([Hearing Review](https://hearingreview.com/hearing-products/hearing-aids/otc/fda-authorizes-first-otc-hearing-aid-software)).

## 7. Naming, positioning, category, onboarding

Names (not trademark-checked; "Whisper"/"Whisperr" and "Chatable" are taken): **Sotto**, **Earshot**, **Tablemate**, **Nearvoice**, **Huddle**, **Duet Talk**. Positioning: "Talk normally in loud places — your AirPods, their AirPods, no signal needed." Category: Utilities (primary), Lifestyle or Health & Fitness (secondary); avoid Medical.

Onboarding (5 screens): (1) Value: one sentence + Continue. (2) "Put in your AirPods and switch on Noise Cancellation" with live route detection. (3) Microphone permission, explained in context. (4) Pair: "Hold phones together; one of you taps Pair" → system PIN. (5) "You're live" — partner indicator, mute, and the Conversation Awareness tip.

## End-to-end journey, settings, risks

**First use**: install → onboarding → pair (PIN) → talking within ~60 s → lock phone, Live Activity/CallKit persists → end from island or stem press. **Repeat use**: open app or tap partner in the Live Activity suggestion → auto-reconnect to a remembered partner → talking in one tap.

**Settings (minimal)**: Paired partners (forget); Auto-connect to known partners; Captions on/off; Voice Isolation on/off; link to Headphone Accommodations.

**Top five risks**: (1) End-to-end latency/echo through two Bluetooth hops (>150 ms likely without the HFP route). (2) Background survival: MPC dies when backgrounded and Wi-Fi Aware requires the app "actively running" — CallKit/audio session is the only lifeline. (3) AirPods behaviour outside the app's control: Conversation Awareness flapping, HFP narrowband mic quality, ANC leakage. (4) Platform floor: Wi-Fi Aware needs iOS 26 and iPhone 12+, no Android; MPC fallback inherits the discovery flakiness users blame Bridgefy/Talky for. (5) Airline and review friction: crew policies on Wi-Fi, Local Network purpose-string rejections, and medical-claim wording.
